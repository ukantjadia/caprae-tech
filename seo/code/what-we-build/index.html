How we decide what's worth building.

Rule 1

We decide what to build before we write any code.

A founder works through every feature with you: can it be built, and will someone use it or pay for it?

Rule 2

The first version is smaller than you think.

We cut to the one thing the product has to prove, build that, then grow from evidence.

Rule 3

Daily use comes first.

Features people need every morning go in first. Features they open once a month wait.

Rule 4

We don't rebuild what already works.

If a tool your users already know does it better, we connect to it instead of copying it.

Rule 5

Every feature earns its cost twice.

Once in the hours to build it, and again in what it costs to run, including AI.

Rule 6

Data first, AI last.

We look at your data before we automate anything. AI on top of bad data just makes a mess faster.

Rule 7

A human checks the AI.

Every AI step is graded against a person before you rely on it.

Rule 8

We'll tell you when not to build.

Some of our most useful advice is what to leave out, and when to buy instead.

How a build runs: decide, then build.

Decide
1

You tell us what you want to build

In plain words. The problem, who has it, and what's in the way. No specs needed.

2

The founders' table

Founders from our team sit down with you and pull the idea apart: what to build first, what to leave out, how it will sell, and what it costs to run.

3

A feature list with hours

Every feature gets an hour estimate at one fixed rate. You decide what's in.

Build
4

Prove the hard part

A proof of concept, or a data review for dashboards and AI. You see it work before you fund the rest.

5

Build in pieces, check every piece

Each part is built and tested on its own, then joined into one product. AI steps are graded against a human.

6

Launch, then help it sell

We decide what goes on top and how each feature is presented, and can help you find your first users.

What makes a build good or bad.

Worth building

When it…

  • Gets used every day, not once a month
  • Saves more than it costs to run
  • Does something no existing tool does well
  • Can be checked by a person
Not worth building (yet)

When it…

  • Copies a tool people already use and like
  • Costs more in tokens and servers than it saves
  • Needs data you don't have yet
  • Looks good in a demo but nobody will use it
